# Novel insights into conserved biomineralization mechanisms revealed from a cold-water scleractinian coral skeletal proteome

### Abstract

Stony corals exhibit striking morphological plasticity across diverse ecological contexts and trophic modes, raising fundamental questions about conservation of their biomineralization machinery. Here, we characterize the skeletal organic matrix (SOM) of the cosmopolitan cold-water, asymbiotic coral *Desmophyllum pertusum* compared with proteomes of a facultatively photosymbiotic temperate coral and an obligately photosymbiotic subtropical coral. Across this spectrum, we observe convergence on a shared “biomineralization toolkit,” including evidence for extracellular protein transport via vesicle-mediated and cytoskeletal pathways. Newly predicted architectures expand the repertoire of acidic proteins, suggesting additional roles in stabilizing amorphous calcium carbonate and buffering protons within specialized vesicles. Together, our proteomic and structural analyses refine the stony coral biomineralization model from a static list of matrix components to a coordinated network of pathways and establish *D. pertusum* as a model for dissecting the molecular mechanisms underlying coral calcification, the basis of “vital effects”, and their sensitivity to ocean change.
